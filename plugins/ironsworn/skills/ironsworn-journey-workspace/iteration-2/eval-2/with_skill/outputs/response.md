## Waypoint Narration

The ridge comes at you hard by mid-afternoon — a long grey switchback of loose shale that demands your full attention with every step. You pick your way up it carefully, one hand brushing the rock face for balance, and when you crest the top, Deepcrag is not yet in sight. It won't be for a while. But from this vantage you can see the lay of the land ahead: a dense band of pine forest threading down into a fog-filled valley, and beyond it, the dark suggestion of higher ground.

You stop to breathe and check your pack. The food is lighter than you'd like. You've been burning through it faster than you planned — a missed shot at a deer two days back, a water skin that leaked into your hardtack. You eat a small portion now, just enough to keep your legs under you, and press on before the light changes.

Progress is real. But the road ahead is longer than what's behind.
